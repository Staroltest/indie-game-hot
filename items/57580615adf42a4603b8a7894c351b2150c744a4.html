“我们不想要一个满心复仇的阴郁英雄”：安静的间隙与爱画鸟的主角，让 Control Resonant 成为更内省的动作游戏

原文标题："We didn't want the brooding hero out for revenge": How quiet lulls and a bird-sketching lead make Control Resonant a more introspective kind of action game

导读

Control Resonant 像它那扭曲的曼哈顿里不断重复的图案一样，让人措手不及：一会儿还在用 Aberrant 对着成群的非人敌人大开杀戒，下一刻却在屋顶上找一只会吱吱叫的紫色龙玩偶，送给一只疑似会传送的、摇着尾巴的小狗。战斗常常惨烈而绝望，但这些安静的间隙让它成为一款更内省的动作游戏。

原文摘要

Like the repeating pattern effect afflicting its warped Manhattan, Control Resonant threw me for a loop. One minute I would be brutally slashing and smashing my way through hordes of inhuman enemies, enacting a never-ending chain combo of bellowing violence with my Aberrant, and the next minute I would be scanning nearby rooftops for signs of a squeaking purple dragon plushie to give to a very wiggly dog (who, I was pretty sure, could teleport). Combat often felt desperate and bloody; knockdown-and-drag-out brawls that had me limping away like John Wick after a particularly messy gunkata affair. Still, these fights all ended eventually, and in their place was not always more action-RPG battling but, more often, an odd sense of melancholic peace and quiet. It’s a strange dichotomy I’ve been thinking about nearly nonstop since I finished my first playthrough. Spoilers/Revelations for Control Resonant ahead. Don’t say we didn’t warn/notify you.
